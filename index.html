How it works

Your path to the final

Expect 300–400 registrations. Only 30–50 teams reach the hall, so make your first round count.

  1. Register

    Sign up online with your team members, college, district, skills and idea.

  2. Round 1: online screening

    Submit a one-page idea and a 2-minute video. A panel scores every entry.

  3. Shortlist

    30–50 teams are selected, with a waitlist of 10 in case of drop-outs. Results go to all applicants.

  4. Confirm and prepare

    Selected teams confirm by a set date and receive data packs and preparation material.

  5. Hackathon: Day 1

    Twelve hours on the university campus, on real problems, with mentors in the room.

  6. Day 2 and beyond

    Top teams present at the expo, then continue into pilots and incubation.

On the day

Inside the 12 hours

Hour 0Hour 6Hour 12
  1. Start

    Check-in and briefing

    Collect your team kit, then walk through the problem statements.

  2. All day

    Mentor rounds

    Mentors on a 2-hour rotation move through the hall.

  3. Hour 6

    Progress check

    See where your team stands, with time left to change course.

  4. Hour 12

    Final submission

    All work goes in a new code repository started that day.

  5. Finish

    Judging and results

    Judges have no link to any team. Results and certificates follow.

We take care of the rest: tables, power points, Wi-Fi, a help desk, and food and water for the full 12 hours.

FAQ

Good questions

When is the hackathon?

Three dates are proposed: 11–12 Dec 2026, 18–19 Dec 2026, or 8–9 Jan 2027. The hackathon is Day 1 of the summit. The final date will be announced here.

How big can a team be, and is there a fee?

Team size and the team fee will be announced with the rules, before registration opens.

Do we have to be from Vidarbha?

No. The hackathon is built around Vidarbha’s problems, and the shortlist keeps a fair mix of Vidarbha and other regions.

What if someone on our team is under 18?

Under-18 participants need parent consent. Selected teams also complete a photo consent form.

Can we start building before the day?

You can prepare your idea, and data packs are sent to selected teams in advance. All submitted work must be in a new code repository started on the day.

What if we land on the waitlist?

A waitlist of 10 teams is kept in case of drop-outs. If a place opens, a waitlisted team can move up.

Who owns what we build?

IP terms will be set out in the participant rules, published alongside the code of conduct.
